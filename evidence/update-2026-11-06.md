# 6 November: functional synthetic redraw succeeds; real-scene bridge remains open

Evidence cutoff: **2026-11-06 10:54 UTC**, through reviewed R213. [Machine-readable observations E44–E50](update-2026-11-06.json) bind curated facts to private source-report hashes. The later read-only palace-context draft and any subsequent outcomes are excluded. These are local Metal observations in Unity 6000.3.26f1 / URP 17.3.0, not Quest measurements. No private renders or source geometry are published.

## A useful far-range milestone, limited to one view — E44

The broader far-range trial produced two summits, a saddle and a descending shoulder in place of the blunt mound. Its far-only comparison changed **28,278 pixels** with **zero protected-foreground residuals**, unchanged water/foot comparisons and exact repeats. The forms remained broad and smooth against the reference; this was not final art acceptance.

The sixteen-image trial also tested nearer-slope changes. Those near-only and combined arms still had **31 residuals**, pale/patchy appearance and a conspicuous foot seam. The overall trial remained FAIL_PROTECTED. A local far-only result does not erase the near failure, prove every viewing direction or recover the missing historical baseline.

## Native controls closed two misleading repair routes — E45–E47

An unchanged material clone was exact, while replacing its texture path retained 31 foreground residuals. This rejects material-instance identity alone as the explanation in that test; texture content, resolution and derived binding were not separated.

An exact-order owned mesh copy then changed **32 protected pixels**. A partitioned copy changed the same 32 residual pixels plus 74 allowed-region pixels. Original restoration was exact. That result retired the whole copied-draw route as a neutral near repair. Byte-equal geometry is not equivalent rendering context.

Later synthetic controls exposed harness-specific blockers before the candidate could be judged. A fractional clear differed by one blue code value before the near feature registered. An Editor offscreen MSAA route failed an overly specific backbuffer-identity condition. A hardcoded depth-format condition refused the platform default. Zero-change controls before pass execution were correctly treated as vacuous for redraw identity. Fixture-owned corrections received bounded review; these results do not authorize global AA/depth changes or relaxed product comparisons.

A subsequent direct original-material lit redraw changed **66,385 pixels** despite exact repeats and restoration. Unity's [DrawRenderer documentation](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/rendering/commandbuffer/drawrenderer) explains that this command does not prepare lighting-related shader inputs. That is a relevant API limitation, not a unique proof of every pixel's cause. The next concrete route used renderer-list/culling context rather than another geometry or colour adjustment.

## Fixture isolation is a controlled change of reference — E48

Renderer-list trials first stopped on shadow guards. Shadow capability flags did not mean the fixture's light itself cast shadows; a later observation did report a visible shadow-casting light even though the owned light was configured otherwise. An external contributor was plausible, not uniquely identified by those observations.

The reviewed correction leased scene/camera culling masks only on owned objects. It retained existing lights and global lighting settings, conservative foreign-object coverage, explicit restoration and actual scene-unload testing. The [Camera API](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/camera/overridescenecullingmask) describes scene-based culling; it does not certify isolation of every lighting input or hidden engine scene.

Crucially, the isolation-off control differed from the isolated reference by **826 pixels**. The isolation-on repeat was exact. The new run therefore tests the isolated fixture against its own reference. It does not convert the earlier 66,385-pixel failure into a same-baseline repair, nor prove that an external light was its only cause.

## Actual functional and scene-unload success — E49

The isolated run completed **28 observations**. Before this public curation, the review thread rechecked every saved raw-frame hash and full length, all **16 byte-exact RGBA control comparisons**, and five changed-pixel counts.

The meaningful results were:

- Original-material and owned-material identity redraws were exact, including the statically batched case.
- Key and Active changed **45,250** and **24,300** pixels per case, with zero protected/masked hits; Key missed no required target pixels. This rules out an inert pass as the explanation for the identity result in this fixture.
- The second camera stayed unchanged, and unsupported producer cases correctly became whole-frame no-ops.
- All **20** mask readbacks were issued, consumed and retired, with at most one live request, none remaining and zero mask mismatch pixels.
- Ten near-pass records executed on the tested existing-attachment route. Genuine scene unload completed, with zero cleanup faults, live instances or subscriptions afterwards.

This is a real functional rendering/lifecycle result. It is not finished landscape artwork. The final static-case light snapshot showed one owned, non-shadow-casting visible light and the NoCapability shadow branch. It does not establish every frame's light census, full lighting ownership, the empty-default shadow branch or real-shadow palace support.

**Memory remained UNKNOWN across ten rows. Overall verdict: INVALID; NUnit: Failed.** A specifically released diagnostic protocol allowed functional evidence collection without pretending those unknown costs passed the product budget. Neither renderer-list native storage nor induced attachment costs become known because the pictures look correct. The product's 32 MiB allowance remains unchanged.

## The remaining bridge is an evidence question — E50

The actual palace has not yet passed the synthetic identity/active protocol. Its configured shadow-casting sun, actual frame shadow capability, eligible producer count and camera/resource route must be distinguished. The prototype census holds sixteen records, including two target records, and is not simply a frustum-limited production census.

An attachment scenario of **58,982,400 bytes** was an unverified assumption. It was corrected as neither measured storage nor a lower bound, so it cannot prove permanent over-budget behavior or impossibility of verification. The available original scene and generated variant also need distinct baseline labels.

The next bounded proposal is a read-only actual-scene context check. No later result is claimed here. The 31 near-texture failures, 32 copied-draw residuals, earlier direct-redraw failure, reconstructed-baseline gaps and full-view/stereo/device requirements remain visible.

For reuse without the entire history, read [lit redraw and fixture isolation](../skills/unity-urp-render-debugging/references/lit-redraw-and-fixture-isolation.md). The main transferable lesson is to establish non-vacuous identity rendering in the correct lighting and attachment context, retain changed-baseline controls, and report functional success separately from resource and product acceptance.
